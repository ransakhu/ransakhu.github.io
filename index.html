01

ezibda

A macOS app that organizes a photo library by meaning — running entirely on-device.

Role
Project management · UX · ML integration
Platform
macOS · SwiftUI · CoreML · MobileCLIP S2 · Xcode · Figma
View on the App Store ↗

Photographers accumulate thousands of unlabeled shots. ezibda runs MobileCLIP S2 through CoreML directly on the Mac, making a library searchable by meaning — no upload, no cloud, no subscription.

I led the project and owned the research and ML integration: a 100-response survey, 10+ interviews, 5 usability sessions, and a field study at Orb Studio with photographer Saleh Alsaqabi, whose culling workflow shaped the final UX.

02

Papricka

A sourdough starter tracker — feeding logs, photo history, and archive.

Role
Design & development
Platform
iOS · SwiftUI · Xcode Cloud · TestFlight · App Store Connect
View on the App Store ↗

Keeping a sourdough starter is a ritual of small, repeated observations. Papricka turns that into a log: timestamped feedings, photo capture, and an archive view showing how a starter changes over weeks.

Grounded in community research and 5 user interviews, then shipped through an Xcode Cloud → TestFlight → App Store pipeline I set up and ran.

03

Ratabah

A bilingual focus and task app built for ADHD.

Role
Design & SwiftUI development
Platform
iOS · SwiftUI · Arabic / English localization
View on the App Store ↗

Most task apps assume you can decide what to do next. For ADHD users that assumption is the barrier. Ratabah strips the interface down to the fewest possible decisions between opening the app and starting work.

I owned several of the core SwiftUI screens and grounded the design in a field study with an ADHD support association, plus interviews and testing with 8+ users. Fully localized in Arabic and English.

04

Abyan

End-to-end UX for a Shariah-compliant investment app.

Role
UX research & product design
Platform
iOS concept · Figma · Prototyping
View on Behance ↗

Investing apps intimidate first-time users, and Shariah-compliant investing adds a layer of rules most interfaces never explain. Abyan is my attempt to make both legible.

I interviewed Abyan's product manager and analyzed user reviews and tester feedback, then carried the product from problem framing through information architecture to high-fidelity prototypes.
